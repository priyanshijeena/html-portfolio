Reviews of My Recent Watched

By Priyanshi

White Nights

The 1959 Soviet film White Nights (directed by Ivan Pyryev) is a visually stunning and poetic adaptation of Fyodor Dostoevsky's classic novella. It captures the melancholic, ethereal romance of unrequited love and the surreal atmosphere of St. Petersburg. The movie captured the emotions, atmosphere, and beauty of the original story really well. It stayed true to the book's themes and brought the characters to life in a way that felt genuine.

White Nights is a deeply moving and melancholic adaptation of Dostoevsky's novella. More than a simple love story, it is a reflection on loneliness, hope, and the human desire to be understood. Set against the dreamlike backdrop of St. Petersburg's white nights, the film creates an atmosphere that feels both magical and heartbreaking. The story follows a lonely dreamer whose life changes when he meets Nastenka, a young woman waiting for the return of the man she loves. Over a few nights, the two share their thoughts, fears, and dreams, forming a connection that feels genuine and intimate. What makes the film so powerful is its understanding of emotions that many people experience but rarely express—the longing for companionship, the comfort of being seen, and the pain of loving someone who cannot love you back in the same way. The performances are sincere and emotionally rich, bringing warmth and vulnerability to the characters. The cinematography beautifully captures the quiet streets and luminous nights, creating a romantic yet bittersweet atmosphere that perfectly matches the story's tone. Every scene feels thoughtful, allowing the audience to sit with the characters' emotions rather than rushing through them. While the pacing may feel slow to modern viewers, that slowness is part of the film's charm. It gives space for reflection and allows the emotional weight of the story to build naturally. The ending, in particular, is unforgettable—heartbreaking without being cruel, and sad without losing its sense of beauty. It reminds us that even brief connections can leave a lasting impact on our lives.
Overall, White Nights is a timeless and emotionally resonant film that explores love, loneliness, and the fragile nature of human dreams with remarkable sensitivity. It is a quiet masterpiece that lingers in the mind long after the final scene, making it a rewarding watch for anyone who appreciates thoughtful, character-driven storytelling.

Sanam Teri Kasam

It's a rewatch; I love this movie so much It's literally the best movie I've ever watched. Sanam Teri Kasam (2016) is an intense, emotional Bollywood tragedy. 9/10 for the writing, the hype is totally justified. The visuals are great too, and yeah, there are flaws and stereotyping, but honestly, who cares? It was way ahead of its time. Films like this deserve appreciation, and I'm glad it got the love it deserved with the re-release.
Would obviously re-watch it again.

Sanam Teri Kasam is one of those rare romantic films that fully embraces emotion and heartbreak. Directed by Radhika Rao and Vinay Sapru, the film tells the story of Saru, a kind-hearted and intelligent young woman who constantly faces rejection because she doesn't fit society's conventional standards of beauty. Her life changes when she crosses paths with Inder, a misunderstood man carrying his own emotional wounds. What begins as an unlikely friendship slowly grows into a deeply moving love story. The film's greatest strength lies in its emotional sincerity. Rather than relying on grand gestures alone, it focuses on the vulnerability of its characters and the way they help each other heal. Mawra Hocane delivers a heartfelt performance as Saru, portraying her transformation with warmth and authenticity, while Harshvardhan Rane brings quiet intensity and depth to Inder. Their chemistry feels natural, making the emotional moments all the more impactful. The soundtrack is another standout element. Songs like Sanam Teri Kasam and Tera Chehra beautifully complement the story, enhancing both its romantic and tragic moments. The music doesn't just accompany the narrative—it becomes part of its emotional core. Visually, the film creates a dreamy and romantic atmosphere, with elegant cinematography and a soft aesthetic that matches its tone. While some viewers may find certain plot developments melodramatic and the emotional intensity overwhelming, the film never hides what it wants to be: a classic tragic romance designed to make the audience feel deeply. What makes Sanam Teri Kasam memorable is its exploration of unconditional love, acceptance, and sacrifice. It reminds viewers that love is not always about happy endings; sometimes it is about the way another person changes your life forever, even if they cannot stay in it. Overall, Sanam Teri Kasam is a heartfelt, emotionally charged romance that resonates with anyone who enjoys tragic love stories. Though not without its flaws, its sincere performances, beautiful music, and powerful emotional impact have helped it earn a devoted fan following over the years. It is a touching and unforgettable romance that leaves a lasting ache long after the credits roll.

Pride and Prejudice(1995)

The 1995 Pride and Prejudice is a highly acclaimed 6-episode BBC television miniseries. Adapted by Andrew Davies from Jane Austen's 1813 classic It is widely celebrated for its sharp wit, lavish period production, and faithfulness to the original novel. It's much more subtle- You have to closely watch everyone's faces to see what they are thinking, which is exactly how it used to be. Back then, etiquette and behavior were rigid. That's part of the tension. People could not just express how they felt, especially in terms of strong emotions. That's why it is so brilliant. The emotions are there, but you have to look for them. You feel like you are in the room with them, trying to decode what's going on behind the rigid mask they are all forced to wear. It's definitely better than the 2005 version.

Pride and Prejudice (1995) is widely regarded as one of the finest adaptations of Pride and Prejudice ever made. Rather than simply retelling the story, the miniseries captures the wit, charm, and social commentary that make Austen's novel so beloved. Across six episodes, it allows the characters and relationships to develop naturally, giving the story an emotional depth that many shorter adaptations cannot achieve. At the heart of the series are the unforgettable performances of Jennifer Ehle as Elizabeth Bennet and Colin Firth as Mr. Darcy. Ehle brings intelligence, warmth, and quiet confidence to Elizabeth, making her both relatable and admirable. Firth's portrayal of Darcy is equally remarkable, perfectly balancing pride, awkwardness, vulnerability, and sincerity. Their chemistry develops gradually, making their eventual romance feel earned rather than inevitable. The series excels at portraying the complexities of family, class, reputation, and marriage in Regency England. Every character, from the ridiculous Mr. Collins to the dramatic Mrs. Bennet, feels vividly realized. Even supporting characters receive enough attention to become memorable, adding richness and humor to the story. Visually, the miniseries is stunning. The grand estates, elegant costumes, and picturesque countryside create an immersive period atmosphere without overshadowing the characters. The cinematography and score enhance the romantic mood while preserving the novel's sharp humor and irony. One of the adaptation's greatest strengths is its pacing. By taking its time, it allows viewers to fully understand the misunderstandings, prejudices, and personal growth that drive the story. Elizabeth and Darcy's journey from mutual misjudgment to genuine respect and love remains one of literature's most satisfying romances, and this adaptation presents it beautifully. More than a romance, Pride and Prejudice is a thoughtful exploration of character, social expectations, and personal growth. The 1995 miniseries captures all of these elements with remarkable care, making it a timeless watch for both longtime Austen fans and newcomers. Elegant, witty, romantic, and endlessly rewatchable, Pride and Prejudice remains the gold standard for Austen adaptations and one of the greatest period dramas ever made.

Love Rosie

Love, Rosie (2014) is a charming but frustrating romantic comedy-drama that thrives on the electric chemistry of its leads, Lily Collins and Sam Claflin. While visually appealing and warm, the film suffers from a cliché-heavy, miscommunication-driven plot that spans over a decade of missed opportunities. The tone was pretty serious for a rom-com. The story was really bittersweet and there are couple of scenes where it really touched me and I really felt bad for both characters. It's funny, frustating and warm at the same time.

Love, Rosie is a charming and emotionally engaging romantic film that explores the idea that timing can be just as important as love itself. Based on Where Rainbows End, the story follows Rosie and Alex, two best friends whose lives remain intertwined despite years of missed opportunities, misunderstandings, and unexpected turns. The film's greatest strength is the chemistry between Lily Collins and Sam Claflin. Their performances make Rosie and Alex feel genuine and relatable, capturing the comfort, affection, and frustration that come with a friendship that constantly hovers on the edge of becoming something more. Their connection is so natural that viewers find themselves rooting for them from the very beginning. What makes Love, Rosie stand out from many romantic comedies is its willingness to embrace life's messiness. Rather than following a straightforward romance, the film explores how dreams, responsibilities, mistakes, and circumstances can shape a person's path. Rosie and Alex repeatedly find themselves separated by bad timing and difficult choices, creating a story that is as much about growing up as it is about falling in love. The film balances humor and heartbreak effectively. There are plenty of lighthearted and genuinely funny moments, but it also isn't afraid to explore disappointment, regret, and the pain of wondering "what if." These emotional highs and lows make the story feel more realistic and emotionally satisfying. Visually, the film has a warm and inviting atmosphere, with beautiful settings and a soundtrack that complements its romantic tone. While some plot developments can feel a little predictable and rely on familiar romantic-comedy tropes, the sincerity of the characters keeps the story engaging throughout. At its core, Love, Rosie is a story about friendship, timing, and the enduring nature of love. It reminds viewers that some relationships can survive years of distance, mistakes, and missed chances, and that sometimes the right person has been there all along. A warm and lovable friends-to-lovers story with excellent chemistry, emotional depth, and a message that resonates with anyone who has ever wondered whether love can survive bad timing.

Five Feet Apart

Also a rewatch. You would've already guessed I'm all in for a good rom-com movie,with a sad ending (eventhough I personally don't count it as tragic as both of them seperated on their own accord and didn't die exactly. It's sad yes but not tragic exactly). Five Feet Apart is a heartfelt, emotional romantic drama that centers on two teens with Cystic Fibrosis who fall in love but must stay physically separated to survive. CF patients have to stay at least six feet from each other wiht latex gloves, and no touching at all times. As Stella falls for Will she says, “After all CF has taken from me, I don't mind stealing one foot back.” And so they have a date, still within the walls of the hospital using a five-foot pool cue to measure their distance. Hence the name Five-feet Apart!!

Five Feet Apart is a heartbreaking yet hopeful romantic drama that explores love, illness, and the painful reality of wanting to be close to someone when circumstances make it impossible. While it follows some familiar young-adult romance tropes, the film succeeds because of its emotional sincerity and the genuine connection between its two leads. The story follows Stella and Will, two teenagers living with cystic fibrosis who meet during their hospital treatments. Because of the risk of cross-infection, they must remain physically apart, making even the smallest gestures of affection impossible. What begins as playful banter gradually develops into a deep and meaningful relationship, forcing them to confront the limits that their illness places on their lives. Haley Lu Richardson delivers a touching performance as Stella, balancing determination, vulnerability, and optimism with remarkable authenticity. Cole Sprouse brings charm and emotional depth to Will, creating a character who feels far more complex than the typical romantic lead. Together, they share a chemistry that makes every moment feel meaningful, especially because their love story is built on emotional intimacy rather than physical closeness. One of the film's greatest strengths is its ability to make ordinary moments feel significant. A simple touch, a shared glance, or standing one step closer carries enormous emotional weight. The title itself becomes a powerful symbol of the sacrifices Stella and Will must make to protect each other. Visually, the film is beautifully shot, with soft cinematography and a moving soundtrack that enhance its emotional impact. While some viewers may find parts of the story overly sentimental, the film's emotional honesty helps it avoid feeling manipulative. It captures both the joy of falling in love and the frustration of having that love restricted by circumstances beyond one's control. Beyond the romance, Five Feet Apart is also about learning to live fully despite uncertainty. It explores grief, friendship, family, and the difficult balance between protecting yourself and truly living your life. The film encourages viewers to appreciate the people around them and not take human connection for granted. Overall, Five Feet Apart is an emotional and memorable story that blends romance with themes of resilience and hope. It may leave you in tears, but it also leaves behind a powerful message about love, courage, and making every moment count. A beautiful and bittersweet romance that proves love isn't measured by how close you can stand to someone, but by how deeply you care for them.
